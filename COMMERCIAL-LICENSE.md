# Commercial licensing

**This document is a plain-language explanation, not a legal contract.**
It has not been reviewed by a lawyer. Before offering or selling a
commercial license to anyone, have this reviewed and turned into a real
license agreement by a qualified attorney — the summary below is meant to
capture intent so that work isn't lost, not to be pasted into an invoice or
relied on as a binding document.

## Why this exists

Water Monkey's core is licensed under the [GNU Affero General Public
License v3.0](LICENSE) (AGPLv3). AGPLv3 is real, OSI-approved open source —
anyone can use, modify, and self-host it for free, including commercially.
Its one obligation: if you modify the software and let others interact
with it over a network, you must make your modified source available to
those users too.

Some companies don't want that obligation attached to their business —
they want to use or build on Water Monkey without being required to
publish their own modifications. A commercial license is the alternative
for them: a separate agreement, outside AGPLv3, that removes that
copyleft requirement in exchange for payment.

This dual-licensing approach is only possible because Water Monkey's
copyright is held by its author(s), who can license the same code under
AGPLv3 to the public *and* under different terms commercially, at the
same time. (If the project ever accepts outside code contributions, a
Contributor License Agreement — granting the project broad enough rights
to keep doing this — would need to be in place first; not needed today
for a solo-authored codebase.)

## What a commercial license is intended to cover

- **Freedom from AGPLv3's copyleft obligation** — a paying licensee can
  modify Water Monkey and use it (including offering it as part of their
  own product or internal tool) without being required to publish their
  modifications.
- **Access to Pro-tier features** — functionality that lives outside the
  public AGPLv3 repository (currently: scheduled scans, report export,
  multi-account profiles, and later tiers).
- **No warranty beyond what's explicitly negotiated.** The default
  assumption, matching AGPLv3 itself, is "as-is" — a real commercial
  agreement would need its own explicit warranty/support terms if any are
  offered.

## Pricing — decided 2026-08-23

**No separate price.** Paying for the Pro subscription
*is* how a company gets the commercial license — one purchase grants both
Pro-tier features and freedom from AGPLv3's copyleft obligation. Given the
realistic buyer here is a single senior/lead engineer or a 1–2 person
cloud team, not a large org, a second SKU for "legal cover only, no
features" would serve a customer that essentially doesn't exist for this
product; Pro already covers them.

**The one case this doesn't cover:** a company that wants to **embed,
fork, or resell** the core commercially — meaningfully different from
"use it internally," since it's granting IP/redistribution rights, not
tool access. That case is intentionally **not self-serve and not
publicly priced** — handle it as a negotiated "contact us" deal, the same
way the Enterprise tier already is.

## What this is not

- Not a support contract. Support terms (response time, channels, SLAs)
  would need to be a separate, explicit agreement if offered.
- Not applicable to the AGPLv3-licensed core itself. Anyone can already
  use that under AGPLv3's own terms, for free, without needing this.
